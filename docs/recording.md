# Recording playbook

These are habits that make the editor's cuts sound natural. The editor follows a dialogue editor's
playbook, but a clean recording is always the best cut.

## When you flub a line

- **Pause for a beat, then restart the whole sentence.** Not the word, not the phrase: the sentence.
  The editor keeps the complete second read and cuts at the pause, which is the cleanest splice there is.
  This is how voiceover pickups are done.
- **Don't comment on it.** No "sorry", no "let me redo that". Just pause and restart. Comments become
  extra material to cut, and they often run straight into the restart.
- **Get a big retake right once.** If a whole paragraph went wrong, read it again from the top. The
  editor picks the best full take of each sentence.

## Fillers

- Short "uh"s in fluent speech are fine. The default edit style keeps them, because over-cleaned speech
  sounds scripted. What gets cut is stalls, restarts and corrections.

## Picture

- **Hands off the table.** Knocks shake the camera. The editor can stabilize a clip (the Stabilize
  switch), but it costs a small zoom.
- **Record landscape 16:9.** In CapCut's teleprompter, set the canvas to 16:9. A 9:16 canvas saves a
  small letterboxed picture. The editor crops it automatically, but the picture is only about 600 px
  tall.
- **Front camera?** If text on your shirt reads backwards, turn on Mirror for that recording.
- Sit where the framing setting says (left third, right third or centre), so graphics have room.
